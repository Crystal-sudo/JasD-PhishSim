Debrief & education

This page explains what made the message suspicious and how to spot the next one.

Indicators in this message

    01

    Inspect the sender

    Check the full email address behind the display name, not just the name shown — look for lookalike domains, extra subdomains, or a mismatch with the organization the message claims to be from.

    02

    Hover before you click

    Hovering reveals a link's true destination. Mismatched domains, URL shorteners, and IP addresses in place of a hostname are all warning signs.

    03

    Notice urgency and fear

    Phrases like "act now," "account suspended," or a countdown timer are designed to make you skip the usual verification steps. Slowing down is itself a defense.

    04

    Never sign in via email links

    Navigate to the service directly by typing the known address or using a saved bookmark, instead of a link in an unsolicited message — real services rarely require it.

    How to report a suspicious message
    1. Use your mail client's "Report phishing" button if available.
    2. Otherwise, forward the message as an attachment to your security/IT team's reporting address.
    3. Do not reply to the sender or click any links first.
    4. Delete the message once it's been reported.
